## The brief
Picenza Group, a Vietnamese investor with a portfolio of hotels, resort villas and apartments, wanted to turn a former office building at 43 Nguyen Thuong Hien (Hai Ba Trung, Hanoi) into rental housing. The building was meant as a prototype for a new kind of living model, with distinctive amenities and resident experiences. Our task was to build the brand positioning and the foundation for its identity.

## Market & area research
I mapped Hanoi’s long-term rental market into three clear tiers: international chains (35–55M VND/month), Vietnamese operators (10–30M), and small private landlords (6–25M). The gap was clear: international chains offer excellent service but rarely feel like home, while local options are cheaper but fall short on quality. In Hai Ba Trung, rents rose around 16% year over year while new supply stayed scarce, and most existing stock was old collective housing or mini-apartments. To ground the desk research, I also interviewed local brokers about who actually rents in the area and why.

## Audience & insights
I built two personas: a young Vietnamese office couple looking to move out of a cramped old apartment, and an Italian NGO worker who wanted to live close to local life rather than in the bar-filled expat bubble of Tay Ho. Both shared one need: a place that’s comfortable, central, quiet, and fairly priced.

## The core insight
Most rentals offer either a bare room or a “forced” community that feels staged. But residents don’t need a community built for them. They are the community.

## Reference models & brand architecture
I studied Build-to-Rent (Grainger, Weave Living), co-living (Hmlet, Cove) and space management models, then recommended an Endorsed Brand architecture: an Urban Co-living line under Picenza Real Estate, so each building keeps its own personality while carrying the parent brand’s credibility as the portfolio grows.

## Positioning
Using the Brand Key model, we landed on “Where community naturally grows”, a living space where every resident is a unique individual who brings something of their own to the community. The brand personality combines the Creator and the Caregiver: thoughtful care paired with spaces that spark creativity.

## Naming
Four name directions followed from the positioning: TỔ Co-living (meaning both “nest” and “group” in Vietnamese), NEST Co-living, The Slice Co-living (“a slice of life” in the middle of the city), and Góc nhỏ Co-living (“a little corner”).
